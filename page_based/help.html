1. Getting started

The app is a folder of static files. It needs a local web server — opening index.html from the file system will not work, because the browser blocks the data fetches.

cd ~/Work/Reports/PO_Slack/page_based
python3 -m http.server 8080

Then open http://localhost:8080/. Leave the terminal running; Ctrl+C stops the server.

First paint takes a few seconds: the app loads four data files and then draws the first source PDF (52 MB) into the reading area.

2. What is in the record

Four releases, each from a different archive. They are merged into one timeline, but each keeps its own provenance, and the colour of a document always tells you which medium it came from.

ReleaseMediumEntriesPeriodColour
Rand Paul — Slack Part 1 (screenshots)Slack1,2561 Feb – 30 Apr 2020 blue, lighter shade
Rand Paul — Slack Part 2 (export)Slack11,35130 Apr 2020 – 28 Jun 2023 blue, darker shade
FOIA — Proximal_Origin_Emails.pdfEmail10031 Jan – 27 Jul 2020 green
FOIA — SSCP-Drafts-of-Proximal-Origin.pdfManuscript drafts201 Feb – 5 Mar 2020 amber

That is 12,727 entries in total, spanning January 2020 to June 2023.

Read the shades this way: the hue is the medium (blue = Slack, green = email, amber = manuscript draft) and the shade is the release. Rand Paul's two Slack tranches are therefore two shades of the same blue. The FOIA productions are all green, and each new FOIA release joins as another shade of green rather than a new colour.

Seven entries are release notes (grey) — Slack's own "channel created / joined" furniture. They are publisher metadata about the release, not documents in it, so they are kept separate and can be filtered out.

3. The screen and its three rails

Left to right (landscape) there are three collapsible rails and three work areas:

AreaWhat it does
📖 P.O. guideThe sections of the article, and the documents each one rests on.
🔖 BookmarksYour own saved pointers into the record.
📊 Dual timelineEntries and search hits over time.
ResultsThe matched boxes.
PDF panelThe source document, at the page the box came from.

Collapsing and resizing

Every rail and panel collapses by clicking its header (the chevron flips to point back). A collapsed rail stays a thin 36 px strip with its title turned sideways, so it can always be opened again — there is no state you can get stuck in. All three rails and both timelines are open by default.

Two drag handles resize the work areas: the one between the results and the PDF panel (width in landscape, height in portrait), and the one under the dual timeline.

Landscape and portrait

The Switch to… button in the toolbar flips the whole arrangement. The time axis turns with it: vertical in landscape, horizontal in portrait. The bookmarks rail follows the same convention — a vertical list down the left in landscape, a horizontal strip across the top in portrait, with its scroll arrows turning from ▲▼ to ◀▶ to match.

4. Searching

One box searches everything at once — Slack, the FOI'd emails and the manuscript drafts together.

TypeMeans
"blue car"exact phrase (double or single quotes)
blue greenboth words, any order (same as blue AND green)
blue OR greeneither word (same as blue|green)
(a OR b) AND cgrouping with parentheses
bun*wildcard inside a term

The Aa case sensitive checkbox switches from "any capitalisation" (the default) to exact capitalisation. Press ? in the search box for the short search help.

Narrowing by medium

The Sources button filters by medium — Slack, Email, Manuscript draft, Release note. The filter applies to the results, to the counts on the timeline, and to the totals line at once. It is remembered between visits.

Jumping to a date

Jump to date: opens the entries nearest that day (the nearest previous entry when the exact day has none). Useful for moving through the record without a search term.

5. Reading a box

Each result is a card: a header line (date, sender, time, source badge, medium badge, hit count), any chips, the hit navigation bar, and the text itself.

  • Click anywhere in a box to make it active and jump the PDF to its page.
  • ◀ Prev / Next ▶ step through the individual matches inside one box, moving the highlight and the PDF page together — so you can see each hit in the source, not just in the text.
  • 📄 p.N opens the PDF at that page directly.
  • ⤢ Full text lifts the height cap so you can read a long box end to end without scrolling inside it.
  • 🔖 bookmarks the box (see §9).
  • Chips under the header are live: a thread chip opens the message being replied to, a 📎 chip opens the attachment a message carried.
Clicking an email or a manuscript draft loads that release's PDF into the reading area automatically, at the cited page — you never have to pick the source yourself.

6. Scrolling inside a box

A long message is capped in height, and the box scrolls internally. Because the results list itself also scrolls, two scrollbars on screen at once is confusing — so every box has its own always-visible draggable scrollbar down its right edge.

  • Drag the bar, or use the wheel, to move within one box.
  • A small ↕ scroll inside this box for more text hint appears when a box is clipped.
  • Clicking the box's scrollbar never jumps the PDF.
  • PageUp/PageDown page the box under the pointer; the ↑/↓ keys walk the list of matches instead.

7. The dual timeline

Two charts sharing one time axis. On the left (bottom in portrait): all entries per day. On the right (top): search hits per day.

Reading the entries side

Each day's bar is stacked. The colour of a segment is the medium and the shade is the release, exactly as in the table in §2 — so a tall blue bar is Slack, and you can see at a glance whether a busy day was Part 1 or Part 2. A media that is absent from a day simply has no segment there.

The legend

Below the chart, one entry per stacked segment with its colour, name and total. Click a swatch to show or hide that segment; it greys out when hidden. Hovering a legend entry gives its exact count.

Zooming and selecting

  • Scroll over the chart to zoom the time axis; double-click to reset.
  • Click a hit bar on the right to jump to that day's matches.
  • Click an entry bar to open that day's entries (and clear any search).
  • Zoom is never carried across a layout switch, because the axis turns 90°.

8. The P.O. guide rail

The left rail is the index of the article itself — its 214 dated sections — so any message can be read against the claim it supports.

Click a section and the detail box below lists what that section rests on: one line per citation, in alphabetical order by document, each showing the medium (in that medium's colour), the page, and the date.

  • Click a document name — where the app holds that PDF, it opens in the reading area at the cited page. Where it does not, the line stays a label with a Drive ↗ link to the public copy.
  • show that day jumps the results to that date.
  • read this section in the piece ↗ opens your article at that section.

The little badges on each section (e.g. slack 12, email 3) count its citations by medium, in the same colours as the timeline — so the guide and the charts are read the same way.

9. Bookmarks

Your own pointers into the record. Click 🔖 on any box to add one; click again to remove it. The rail lists them with the date and the medium.

  • Click a bookmark to jump back to that box, with the PDF at the same page.
  • ✕ removes it.
  • ▲▼ (or ◀▶ in portrait) scroll the list; an arrow greys out at the end.
  • The list is kept in the browser, so it survives a reload with no file involved.

Saving and loading

💾 Save writes the whole list to a named JSON file (po-slack-bookmarks-YYYY-MM-DD.json). 📂 Load reads such a file back and replaces the current list — it asks first, and it refuses a file that is not a bookmark file rather than silently emptying the rail.

Across the other DRASTIC apps. The file format is deliberately generic: every bookmark records which app it belongs to (po-slack, fauci-diary, daszak-calendar) and the URL to reach it, alongside the P.O.-specific fields. So one bookmarks file can hold pointers into the Fauci Diary and the Daszak calendar as well as into this app. This app jumps directly to entries it holds; for a pointer to a sibling app it opens that app's URL. The format is versioned, so a later app can add fields without breaking an older file.

10. Keyboard

Enter in the search box — search Enter in Jump-to-date — go ↑ ↓ — walk the matches PageUp PageDown — page the box under the pointer ← → — previous / next PDF page

The split is deliberate: vertical keys belong to the match list, because that is what you navigate most; paging inside a long box is on PageUp/PageDown; and the horizontal keys are left to the PDF.

11. Known limits

Stated plainly, because they affect what the record can be asked.

  • Time zones are assumptions. The emails are stamped without a zone. Each message's zone is inferred from the sender's own institution (Wellcome → London, NIH → New York, Scripps → Los Angeles, Tulane → Chicago, Sydney → Sydney, Pasteur → Paris) and the assumption is printed on the card — never applied silently. An explicit UTC offset in a stamp always wins.
  • The scans are OCR. The email release ships as page images. Text is recovered at 300 dpi and cleaned, but names and punctuation carry OCR damage. Nothing is invented: where a word could not be recovered it is left as the OCR read it.
  • Slack Part 1 is a screenshot release, so its text is OCR too; Part 2 is a clean export. Messages with no recoverable text are flagged redacted on the card.
  • 51 documents are cited by the article; only 3 are held here as PDFs (Proximal_Origin_Emails.pdf, SSCP-Drafts-of-Proximal-Origin.pdf, and the two Slack parts). The rest carry a Drive link to the public copy and are queued for ingestion.
  • 145 citations are classified "other" (grey) because the classifier does not yet recognise them — many are plainly FOIA email releases. The colour is a gap in the classifier, not a claim about the document.

12. Release notes

v1.2 — bookmarks, guide, provenance

  • 🔖 Bookmarks rail: a third collapsible sidebar (vertical in landscape, horizontal in portrait), with ▲▼ scroll arrows, a 🔖 toggle on every box, and Save/Load to a named JSON file that travels between the DRASTIC apps.
  • 📖 The P.O. guide is now a collapsible rail like the timeline, and its citations are sorted alphabetically by document. Documents the app holds open in the reading area at the cited page; the rest link to the public copy.
  • 📊 Timeline stacked by media × release: colour is the medium, shade is the release, with a clickable legend and hover help. Rand Paul's two Slack tranches are two shades of blue.
  • ✉ Email headers repaired. The OCR read "Cc:" as "Ce:", and a glyph fragment between fields was closing the header early — which stranded Subject: lines in the message body. Subjects are now recovered on 95 of the 100 messages.
  • ✉ Signatures separated. A title block is no longer read as message prose: it is cut off the text and shown as a signature, with its own line returns preserved.
  • ✉ Line returns repaired. Breaks that were an artefact of the scan column width are rejoined; returns the author actually made are kept.

v1.1 — mixed sources, scrolling, the guide

  • Added the FOI'd email release (100 messages, one entry each) and the 20 dated manuscript drafts, merged into the Slack timeline as first-class entries.
  • Every box got an always-visible draggable scrollbar and a ⤢ Full text expander; macOS overlay scrollbars are invisible at rest, which made long boxes look unscrollable.
  • Fixed the keyboard model: ↑/↓ walk matches, PageUp/PageDown page the box, ←/→ page the PDF.
  • Clicking an email or draft result now loads the correct source PDF automatically.
  • Added a Sources filter (Slack / email / draft / note) across search, browse, timeline counts and totals.

v1.0 — the P.O. Slack record

  • 12,607 Slack messages from the two Rand Paul releases, searchable with the dual timeline and a page-accurate PDF viewer.
